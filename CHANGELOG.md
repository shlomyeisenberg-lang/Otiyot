# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Ghosts and records. Every finished Jaffa Line run, Time Attack and Daily Challenge now takes a place on a board of your five best runs for that exact setting. The next time you play the same setting, your best run comes back as a ghost: on the Jaffa Line a see-through train runs on the other side of the track, and in Time Attack and the Daily a "Ghost" line under the meter counts the words it has finished by now. Beat it and the results card says "A new best!" or your place on the board. The Statistics page has a new Records tab: pick a board and see your own lines and every profile on this device side by side, export any line as a small ghost file to send to a friend, or import a friend's file so their run becomes a ghost you race. A ghost file holds only finish times and numbers, never anything you typed. English and Hebrew.

- Settings and accessibility. Settings now has seven tabs: Display (language, theme, story text speed), Accessibility (colorblind palette, high contrast, large text, dyslexia-friendly font, reduce motion, and new sliders for camera shake and screen flashes, and an option to hide the translation line under story text, which stays on while story lines are read aloud), Graphics (pick a preset or Auto, with a link to the test range), Sound, Typing (now also the difficulty the practice and challenge screens start on), Health and Keys. Health: Dyo reminds you to take a break every 15, 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes (30 by default, off if you like) with a short tip about sitting and hand position that you can also turn off. Keys: choose one more key (F1 to F4 or Pause) that pauses and goes back; Escape always works. Every option changes the game straight away and is kept in your profile; every menu can be tabbed through. English and Hebrew.

- The full story. Before each place opens for the first time, and again when you first restore it, a short chapter plays over the parchment: Reb Ezra, Dyo, Naftali and, in the end, the Keeper talk it through with captions that type out at your text speed (Enter continues, Esc skips). Act Two and Act Three open with their own title cards. Naftali goes from taunting you on the Jaffa Line to standing beside you in the Arena; Reb Ezra learns to let you go ahead of him; the Keeper's scorched pages turn up from the Forge to the Lantern Caves, so by the Blank Scroll you know why he erased the land; Dyo's blotting-paper fears and "a few hundred thousand letters to go" run all the way through. A chapter after a restoration waits behind a button in the House of the Scribe; the Journal has a new Story tab where every chapter you have seen can be watched again. If you turn on "Read story lines aloud", each character has his own pitch and pace. English and Hebrew are written separately, not word for word.

- A polish pass over every place. Reaching a new combo tier now feels the same everywhere and bigger at every step: a halo of sparks in the tier's color (ink, techelet, silver, gold, light) bursts from the letter you just wrote, a ring of light opens, the view leans in, and from silver up the screen glows softly in that color, while the combo ring at the top flares (with reduced motion it only glows). Lessons and Practice now have their own calm music (a music box waltzing over a harp), the Game Room has its own quick accordion tune (it steps aside while a game plays its own sound), and Time Attack, Survival and the Daily Challenge play the music of the place they are set in. The camera no longer rises or dips oddly when you go from one place to another, never sinks into the ground on a hard hit, and stays above the ridges on Hermon. At the Cliffs the waterfalls thin out as you climb past them instead of filling the screen; on the Jaffa Line the train's lamp no longer floods the plain with yellow by day; in the Lantern Caves the word banner rides above the apprentice so he and his lantern are never hidden; and the results card no longer lets its numbers spill over its edge.

- Progression and the Ink Shop. Every run, lesson and mode now earns XP, glyphs and ink that are kept (earlier play counts too). You go up a level every few runs at first; each level gives a skill point, and some give a free gift (a hawk quill at level 2, sepia ink at 3, pennants for the house at 5). The shop shelf in the House of the Scribe opens the Ink Shop: your apprentice as he is dressed now with Dyo beside him, your level and wallet, and four tabs. Shelf: robes (linen, cedar, sea, pomegranate, night sky), quills (hawk, dove, peacock, raven), inks (sepia, techelet, crimson, emerald; Dyo lives in the inkwell, so he takes the ink's color) and things for the house (a brass lamp, a pomegranate tree, pennants, a map of the Land of Letters, wind chimes, a dovecote with two doves); new things come onto the shelf as you level. Crafting: bring a piece you own, hidden letters and ink, and make a gold-leaf quill, a techelet robe, gold ink or a courtyard fountain. Skills: a small tree in three branches with six abilities that change how a run plays (Echo shows one more word ahead, Steady Hand catches a slip per combo tier, Warm Hands starts every run at the second tier, Soft Fall makes a slip cost less, Second Wind brings a broken streak back once with your next perfect word) and three that raise what you earn; you can switch an ability off. Hidden letters: every place hides one letter, found by playing it a particular way, and Dyo whispers a riddle for each. Results cards show what you earned, a new level and any letter you found. Your apprentice wears his robe and quill in every scene, and the decorations stand in the house. English and Hebrew.

- Statistics and the Journal. The main menu gets a Statistics page, and the journal door in the House of the Scribe now opens the Journal (it used to say "soon"). Statistics has five tabs. Overview: sessions, time played, average and best speed, average accuracy, keys mastered, days played and your current streak, with a speed chart and an accuracy chart over your last thirty runs. Keys: how many of the keys you have learned you have mastered, a keyboard heatmap (weaker keys glow red) and the mistakes you make most (the key you meant and the one you hit). Lessons: both lesson tracks for the keyboard you are using, level by level, with lessons passed and stars. History: your fifteen latest runs. Achievements: sixteen small goals (first lesson, ten and fifty runs, an hour played, three days in a row, 20 / 40 / 60 words per minute, a clean run, ten and twenty keys mastered, places restored, rank S, the Ladder), with progress bars. The Journal has three tabs: People (Reb Ezra, Dyo, Naftali and the Keeper, written down as you meet them), Places (every region of the land, opened as the course reaches it) and Tips (typing advice from Reb Ezra, one more written down at each lesson level); entries you have not reached say what opens them. Everything is worked out from the statistics already saved, so nothing new is stored and your old saves just work. English and Hebrew.

- Four ways to play beyond the story, all opened from the practice slate in the House of the Scribe. Practice is a clean drill screen: your weakest keys, what is due for review, any lesson of your course, or your own words (only words made of letters you have learned are used). Time Attack: type as many words as you can before the clock runs out. Survival: your grip tires as you go, finished words give it back and mistakes cost it, and you cross a distance of words before it gives out. Both run in any region you have opened, on any level and difficulty; on Calm there is no clock and the grip never tires. The Daily Challenge gives every date its own scene, level, one or two modifiers and set of words (the same all day), and keeps your best for the date on this device. Challenge modifiers: no lookahead, mirror (words typed backwards), fog, and sudden layout (the keyboard guide vanishes). Everything you type is made only of letters you have learned, every finished run goes into your history and your key statistics, and the course lessons are still one button away. English and Hebrew.

- The Game Room now holds all sixteen games. New Generation: Letter Dash (a three-lane runner), Key Rhythm (notes sliding down each finger's lane), Fortress of Words (castle defence), and the "+" versions of Word Galaxy, Tower of Precision, Key Arena, Grand Prix and Hall of Sefarim, with perks to pick after each level, target letters taken from your weakest keys, bosses and challenges, and coins. Quick Games: Letter Rain, Word Rain and Word Storm (power drops and a thunder-cloud boss). The lobby shows your coins, a booster shop and a daily challenge. Everything you type in them is made only of letters you have learned; scores and coins are kept in your profile.

- The Game Room opens. The door in the House of the Scribe now leads to the arcade lobby, grouped as 3D Classics, New Generation and Quick Games. The five 3D classics play there: Word Galaxy, Hall of Sefarim, Grand Prix, Key Arena and Tower of Precision. They follow your keyboard layout and language and the theme, and every word in them is made only of the letters you have learned; the Arena and the games' letter drills lean on your weakest keys. Each game keeps its settings and best scores in your profile. The other two groups show what is coming.

- The Ladder, endless free play: a tower of letters rising out of a sea of clouds, with no top. Its tiles are carved with the letters you have learned, and a cedar stair winds round it. Every floor is a few words made only of your learned letters (leaning toward the keys you find hardest) and a small challenge from another place: a gale off Hermon that tugs at your grip, a cloud bank that hides the next word, glass rungs that ring on a slip (a clean floor pays double), long words, or a swarm of Blots knocked off the rungs one per word. Every right letter firms your grip and a slip loosens it. On Calm it never gives out, so you rest whenever you like; on the other difficulties it tires as you climb, faster the higher you go: the first time it gives out Dyo catches you, the second time you drop softly into the clouds. Every fifth floor is a landing that gives you your strength back. The world starts grey and gets its color back floor by floor, and higher up the sky turns gold and the first stars come out. Pick modifiers (fog, brittle rungs, long words, a steep tower) for a higher score, and every climb goes into your own table of the ten best. Rank S/A/B/C, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, and its own music on a new marimba voice.

- The Blank Scroll and the Keeper, the end of the story. Once the Silent Arena is restored the map opens the Blank Scroll: an endless strip of white parchment between two great rollers, with no color left in the world. You cross it in five columns, each one the regions you restored in a new form: the Garden (single words), the Workshop (the marks you have learned, where a slip gives ground back to the white), the Race (bursts of words at speed), the Deep (sentences on a swinging tide) and the Dark (one long passage). Every word you type raises a little drawing of a region on the page, and color sweeps over each column when you finish it. At the end stands the Keeper's archive, empty shelves around a great blank page, and the Keeper himself. Naftali and Reb Ezra come to stand with you and call words in turn: type the called word clean and Naftali holds back the Keeper's hand, or Reb Ezra writes back what he wiped. In the last phase the Keeper erases the lines from the end while you write, and you type his own lost words back faster than he can erase them. He is not defeated: you hand him the quill, he writes again, the page turns to gold, the shelves fill with books and color rolls back over the whole land. Then you sign your work by typing the credits, one word for each line, and the profile unlocks New Game+. Learned letters only, Easy / Standard / Hard levels, every difficulty, checkpoints between phases, Hebrew and English, light and night themes, and its own music on a new santur (hammered strings) voice.

- The Trials of the Arena: three more bosses wait in the Silent Arena, each testing a different skill, chosen from a new trials board (keys 1, 2, 3) with each boss's rank. The Storm Hawk (speed) is a thundercloud with a crest of lightning and beating wings that hovers over the stage and strikes in quick bursts: finish each pair of words before the spark lands, then breathe while it rests; the pace climbs every phase and lightning flashes over the theatre. The Glass Golem (precision) is a cut-crystal giant with a glittering crown: it is slow, but every slip mends one of its cracks (it glows and swells back), so a clean hand wins and every perfect word dazzles it. The Long Tide (endurance) is a great sea-green wave with a swaying tail fin: long passages, and a tide that comes fast and then slows, so an even pace carries you to the end. Each has three phases with their own skies, their own section of the boss music, their own lines from Reb Ezra and Dyo, and a short victory cutscene. Beat all three and the Silent Arena is restored (its rank is the lowest of the three) and the way to the Blank Scroll opens. Learned letters only, Easy / Standard / Hard levels, every difficulty, checkpoints, Hebrew and English.

- The Lantern Caves, the first region of Act Three: the old chalk caves under the hills where the scribes once hid their lamps, now full of shadows. You go in with one brass lantern, Dyo floating at your shoulder. Every chamber is one passage of words made only of letters you have learned. Every right letter feeds the flame and every clean streak makes it flare, so the shadows at the edge of the light flinch back; every stumble dims it and they creep one step closer. A bright lantern shows the next words carved on a chalk tablet down the tunnel; a dim one only the word you are typing (on the paper strip too). On Calm the flame never goes out; on the other difficulties it burns down slowly while you type, and if the light goes out the shadows touch you, your streak drops and the lantern is lit again. At the end of every chamber you light an old clay lamp in the wall and the chalk turns white and gold around it. At the end every lamp in the Hall of Lamps catches at once, the spring shines and the shadows melt away. Tense, never frightening. Rank S/A/B/C with flares, times the light went out and lamps lit, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, its own music (a careful tune on a new kalimba voice), the sound of cave drips, and new lantern, shadow and lamp sounds.

- The Long Midbar, the eighth region of Act Two: the old caravan road across the desert to the oasis of the scribes, where the Forgetting has greyed the sand and stirred up the storms. You lead a little caravan of three camels in bright saddle rugs, with Dyo riding on the first one's hump, from well to well. Every stage of the road is two sentences made only of letters you have learned, and every right letter is a step of the caravan. In the first the sky is clear and the next words stand carved on a marker stone ahead of you. In the second a sandstorm rolls in: the sky turns to blowing sand, the road ahead disappears (on Standard and Hard you can only see the word you are typing, and on the paper strip too the next word blurs into the sand) and every word brings a wall of sand rolling in over the dunes. Finish the word and the caravan walks through it as it falls apart into gold dust. On Calm the gusts stop and wait; on the other difficulties a gust that reaches the caravan drops your streak and makes the camels kneel, and comes again, slower. Every storm blows itself out at a well, where the camels drink and the color comes back. At the end the sky clears into a golden evening over the oasis: green date palms, a shining pool, the camels kneeling to drink and a little fire by the tents. Rank S/A/B/C with gusts walked through, times buffeted and wells reached, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, its own music (a rocking 6/8 road song on a new ney, the desert reed flute), and new sounds for the sandstorm, the gusts and the camel bells.

- Atbash City, the seventh region of Act Two: a rainy neon district of code-breakers, where the Forgetting has turned every sign to the backward alphabet (alef for tav, a for z) and shut the vault gates across the street. Every gate is two codes made only of letters you have learned. First the cipher: the gate's sign shows the code in atbash, and every right letter you type flips a neon letter back to its true face. Then the sequence: the vault's tumbler plays a pattern of two codes (like A B A B) and you type it back from memory, one ring of the lock per word. Meanwhile a grey patrol drone sweeps the street with its searchlight; finish before it comes back and you are never seen. On Calm the drones keep their distance; on the other difficulties a drone that comes back first drops your streak and goes round again, slower. Every opened gate sinks into the street, its drone turns into a gold lantern, and the signs and windows come back on block by block, until at the end the whole skyline shines under a purple sky. Rank S/A/B/C with codes done unseen and times spotted, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, its own music (a swung neon tune on a new synth-pluck voice), the sound of rain, and new drone and vault sounds.

- The Star Station, the sixth region of Act Two: high above the land floats the old observatory where the stars are counted, but the Forgetting has dimmed the stars and broken the station apart. The apprentice comes up in a thruster pack and a glass helmet, with Dyo floating beside him in a little bubble. Every module is two sentences made only of letters you have learned: the first fires your thrusters and floats you along an arc to the module's hatch, where you dock and its windows light and its solar wings unfold; the second works the module's turret, where one rock of debris per word drifts in at the hull and finishing the word fires a beam that breaks it into gold dust. Every word lights more stars. On Calm the rocks stop and wait; on the other difficulties a rock that reaches the hull drops your streak and comes in again, slower. At the end the observatory's dome opens, the telescope turns up and four constellations are drawn across the sky over the curve of the land below. Rank S/A/B/C with rocks broken, strikes and stars counted, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, its own music (a slow, weightless waltz with a new glass-harmonica voice) and new thruster and turret sounds.

- Wings over Hermon, the fifth region of Act Two: you ride a great eagle up a green valley to Mount Hermon through a thunderstorm, seen from the rider's seat with the eagle's wings beating at the edges of the picture and Dyo clinging to its neck. The Forgetting has frozen the words into hailstones; they come down out of the clouds one at a time, each carrying a short word made only of letters you have learned. Finish the word before the stone reaches the eagle and it bursts into gold rain that brings the color back to the valley below. The next word waits dark inside the cloud, and only a flash of lightning shows it. On Calm the stones stop in the air and wait; on the other difficulties a stone that lands drops your streak and is thrown back up to fall again, slower. As you go the rain thins and the clouds part, and at the end the evening sun comes out over the snow of Hermon with a rainbow across the valley. Rank S/A/B/C with stones caught in the air and strikes, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, its own music (a soaring 3/4 with a new horn voice and thunder drums) and a new thunder sound.

- The Jaffa Line, after its review: the word you are typing now floats over the track just ahead of your train (before, it hung behind the camera and was never seen), Hebrew words on it read right to left, and it leaves when the line is restored. At the finish the winner is now in front: Naftali's train stops just behind yours when you win, and just ahead when he wins (before, he always stopped behind).

- The Night Shuk, after its review: the punctuation marks you learned in the first lessons now stay learned for good. Before, they dropped out of your learned keys once you reached Level 3, so most players met a market with no marks on the stalls and no coins to win. In Hebrew the word on the floating banner now reads right to left, and the banner leaves when the market is restored.

- The Gematria Forge, after its review: the space between numbers now warms the fire too, and the fire is tuned to a calm, steady pace (Easy holds the green at a careful beginner's speed), so a careful player forges bright gold letters instead of a row of cold ones. Bright letters now earn more experience and glyphs, the results show them as "5/8", and the top chip always says which letter is on the anvil and its value ("Forge g = 7") so the letter values are learned along the way. The number over the anvil disappears when the run ends, and on a phone the results card is no longer covered by the chapter chip.

- The Sunken Geniza, the fourth region of Act Two: a flooded archive hall under green water, grey and still until you type. The old pages lie on stone lecterns along the floor, one short word on each, made only of letters you have learned. The water moves with a tide that follows your own beat, and a ring of light around the lectern breathes with it. Type at an even pace (any pace, as long as it is even) and a current opens that carries the page up whole, in gold, through the light to the surface; a sudden rush of keys stirs the silt and clouds the water, and that page rises slowly instead. Four shafts of light open as you go and the last page restores the hall. Nothing counts down. Rank S/A/B/C with pages carried whole and how steady your tide was, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, and its own music (a slow, swaying 6/8 with a singing cello over a rolling harp).

- Cliffs of Ein Gedi: the path of stepping stones now really goes up and down. Before, English runs climbed to the top and then stayed flat; now top-row and bottom-row words both come up, so you practise reaching both ways.

- The Jaffa Line, the third region of Act Two: a clockwork railway at dawn, from the coast up to the hills, grey and cold until you ride it. Every stretch of track is one short word made only of letters you have learned. Typing drives your train, clean words build boost, and after every third stretch a bend comes: with enough boost you are carried through it and gain on Naftali, who races on the next track in rose and cream; too slow and he pulls ahead. Station lamps light as you go, the colors roll back along the line, and the last stretch restores it. Nothing counts down, and the run always finishes whether or not you win the race. Rank S/A/B/C, Hebrew and English, level and difficulty choices, and its own music (a driving dorian groove with a chugging kanun). The scene also records when each stretch was finished, as data for a later ghost.

- The Night Shuk, the second region of Act Two: a lantern-lit market square after dark, grey and shut until you trade. Every stall is one trade, a learned word with a punctuation mark on its tail once you have learned one, and the marks are the coins: each one jumps into your purse, a clean deal opens the stall's lantern and three clean deals in a row win a haggle that doubles the mark coins. Four big lanterns light over the square as you go and the last trade restores the market. Opens at Level 2 once the Great Blot is beaten, in both languages and on every level and difficulty; only keys you have learned are typed.

- The Gematria Forge, the first region of Act Two: a smithy inside a hill where the furnace has gone out and the smith's rack of letters hangs blank and grey. Every number you type is the value of a letter (alef is 1, yod is 10, kuf 100; in English a is 1, j is 10, s is 100) and the smith forges that letter on the anvil. A tall gauge shows the furnace temperature: every key stokes the fire, a pause cools it, a slip makes it flare. A letter forged while the needle sits in the green comes out bright gold, too cold or too hot it comes out dull or scorched, and nothing can be lost. Four lamps light as you go and the last number restores the cave. Opens once the number row is learned and the Great Blot is beaten, in both languages and on every level and difficulty.

- The Great Blot, the first boss: once the Cliffs are restored, the map opens the Silent Arena, an old stone theatre under a white sky where everything the Fading took has gathered into one huge living blot of ink with pale eyes. Four phases, each different: single words burst the ink globs it flings at you, whole sentences cut its tendrils, a punctuated passage holds back a flood of ink closing in on you, and in the Mirror the world turns inside out while it gives back the words it swallowed, last first. Gold-glinting weak points make it reel. Between phases the camera pushes in, the light changes and the music climbs; a health bar fills with gold as you write it away. On Calm nothing ever reaches you; Calm, Steady and Swift keep a checkpoint at every new phase. Win and it bursts into a rain of gold letters, the arena is restored under a blue sky, and Act Two (the road to the other regions) opens on the map. Only letters you have learned, three levels, five difficulties, a rank S/A/B/C.

- Cliffs of Ein Gedi: the second region can be played once you have learned twelve letters (Play on the map in the house). A grey desert canyon with a line of stepping stones leading to Reb Ezra at the summit: every word is one stone, and a word on the top row makes your scribe reach up to a higher stone, a word on the bottom row down to a lower one, a home-row word a level step. Color sweeps ahead of him as he hops, at the end of each quarter a waterfall starts to flow again and an ibex returns to the ledges, and the last word restores the whole canyon in golden light. Only letters you have learned, no timers on any difficulty, three levels, five difficulties, and a rank S/A/B/C that lights the Cliffs' beacon at home.
- Home Row Meadow: the first region can be played. From the map on the desk, Play takes you to a grey spring valley with a small garden of eight beds, laid out like the home-row keys and colored like the fingers that rest on them. Reb Ezra teaches where the hands go (Esc skips his lesson), and every right letter opens an anemone: in its key's own bed and out through the meadow. Every finished word spreads the green one step farther, birds and butterflies come back, the almond tree blooms, and at the last word the whole valley is restored. Only letters you have learned, no timers on any difficulty, three levels, five difficulties, and a rank S/A/B/C at the end that lights the Meadow's beacon at home.
- The House of the Scribe: the game now has a home. After the opening (or Continue on the title) you arrive at a 3D stone house in the Jerusalem hills, cut away so you can see in, with a courtyard and a view over terraced hills where every region of the Land of Letters keeps a beacon. Every place in the house has a short word made only of the letters you have learned: type it and your apprentice walks there, with Dyo hopping behind. The desk opens the map of the land (what is restored, what is open, the levels with a suggested one, and for a locked region the lesson to learn first), the practice slate opens your next lesson, the trophy shelf shows your ranks, the tool chest opens the settings; the ink shop, the journal and the Game Room say "opening soon". Reb Ezra and Dyo greet you with a line that fits the moment. The day turns while you are home, with lamps at night, fireflies and crickets. Restore a region and its beacon lights and color rolls out across the land. The same places sit in a row at the bottom for the mouse.
- Lessons: the course can now be played. The lesson screen types one lesson with the paper strip and keyboard guide, saves the result (passing at 90% opens the next lesson and new keys), feeds every key to the adaptive engine, and shows accuracy, speed, best streak and any new keys learned.

- Story: the game now opens like a story. Launch shows the title: gold-ink letters fall one by one onto a grey, forgotten parchment, splash ink, bring color back to the page and spell אותיות (OTIYOT beneath), as the camera flies in low across the sheet; any key skips to the choices. "Begin the story" plays the opening of Act 1 in 3D: the lamp-sign over the House of the Scribe goes blank as the street turns grey, Reb Ezra gives you his spare quill, Dyo the ink drop climbs out of the inkwell, and you write the sign's first letter back. Lines type themselves out in a dialogue box with a portrait for each speaker; Enter continues, Esc skips. New settings: story text speed (Display) and reading story lines aloud with your device's own voices (Sound, off by default).
- Combat: the first enemies. Blots, dark ink creatures from the Fading, float in over the test range behind glass word shields with their word on a banner; type a shield's first letter to lock on, and every letter fires a gold ink bolt that cracks the shield until the word shatters it and color floods back where the Blot stood. Five difficulties (Calm with no timers, Steady, Swift, Fierce, and Sofer's Trial where one error ends it), three levels (Easy, Standard, Hard) that open as you learn more letters and always use only the letters you know, a suggested level from your recent fights, mistakes that soften your streak instead of wiping it, and a results card with rank S/A/B/C, XP, glyphs and ink. Try it in the test range's new Wave fight mode.
- Sound: the game now has music and sound, all made in code. Five themes (title, the House of the Scribe, Home Row Meadow, Cliffs of Ein Gedi and a boss battle in three phases) in freygish, minor, dorian and mi sheberach on clarinet, oud, flute, violin, harp, kanun, strings and darbuka. The music builds with your streak: drone and harp at the start, then the melody, the drums, a second voice and busier drums, always changing on the beat. Every key clicks with its own wooden tap and a note that climbs the music's scale as the streak grows; mistakes are a soft low knock. Menus answer with gentle sounds, places have wind, birds, water, fire, crickets or a machine hum, and sounds can come from a point in the world. Settings > Sound has four volumes (music, effects, voice, menu sounds) and opens the new sound room, where every theme and sound can be heard.
- Typing feel: every key now has its moment. A paper strip shows the word letter by letter (the next one in blue, right ones in gold, mistakes in red) with the next word dimmed after it, in correct right-to-left order for Hebrew; a tilted 3D keyboard under the game lights the next key in its finger's color, raises the Shift to hold, dips under every press and can show hands with the finger to use; a combo meter grows and turns from ink to blue, silver, gold and light; WPM and accuracy sit in the corner; rare, kind tips appear for the wrong finger or hands drifting off the home row. In 3D, right keys write the letter in gold with an ink stroke and sparks, wrong keys make the world blink grey for a moment (never blocking), and finished words ring out in gold. Words can stand in the world carved in stone, on a parchment banner or as a hologram. The keyboard guide can be set to keys, keys and hands, or hidden (Settings, Typing).
- 3D engine: the foundation every scene is built on. Four graphics presets (low, medium, high, ultra) with a two-second first-run test that picks the right one for the computer; bloom, light rays, soft shadows, colored fog and a color-restoring sweep that brings a grey world back to life from the typed word; a camera that can follow, ride a rail or sit in a cockpit, with shake, push-in and hit-stop; gold sparks, ink splashes, ink trails, breaking letters and shockwaves; modest robed characters animated by code (idle, walk, run, write, hit, stagger, fall, cheer); an ink-wipe loading transition; and a developer test range at `#/range` that shows all of it with a performance overlay.
- Design system and menus: the parchment and night-ink themes, bundled Hebrew and Latin fonts, a full component set, Hebrew (right to left) and English menus, a first-run welcome that sets up a player with a name, keyboard and lesson track, players, track, keyboard and settings screens, a pause menu, and menus that can be driven by typing each item's gold letter, by arrows and Enter, or by mouse. Display options: theme, colorblind palette, high contrast, large text, dyslexia-friendly font, reduce motion.
